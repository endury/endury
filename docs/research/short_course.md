# Short-Course Triathlon — Sprint and Olympic Distance

Distance-specific training methodology for `sprint_triathlon` and
`olympic_triathlon`. Cited from planner session menus as **`sc §N`** (the
same convention by which menus cite `triathlon_long_course.md` as `tlc §N`,
`endurance_methodology.md` as `em §N`, and `trail_running.md` as `tr §N`).

This document builds on:

- [endurance_methodology.md](endurance_methodology.md) — periodization,
  intensity distribution, fitness modelling, taper science, overtraining.
- [triathlon.md](triathlon.md) — multi-sport fundamentals: session
  interaction, brick training, swim technique economy, weekly architecture.

Only topics requiring short-course-specific treatment are covered here: race
demands by distance, the intensity inversion relative to long course, the
short taper, volume norms, brick and transition specificity, and per-category
planning parameters.

### Epistemic register

The same tiers as `trail_running.md`, applied with the same discipline:

| Tier | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **[PR]** | Peer-reviewed (or a peer-reviewed source read via a citing secondary) |
| **[PC]** | Practitioner consensus — several independent coaching sources agree |
| **[SC]** | Single-coach opinion — one named coach's stated heuristic |
| **[EX]** | Extrapolation by this document — no source states it; the bridge is shown |

Short-course training literature is denser than ultra literature but thinner
than marathon literature: the peer-reviewed base covers race demands and
taper physiology well, while week-architecture numbers are mostly **[PC]**.

---

## 1. Race Demands

Sprint distance (750 m / 20 km / 5 km) finishes in roughly **58-90 min for
trained age-groupers** (over ~75 min for true beginners); Olympic distance
(1.5 km / 40 km / 10 km) in roughly **2:00-3:15** **[PC]**. Two consequences
follow.

**Sprint races are raced near threshold or above throughout.** Race-intensity
studies of short-course events report mean intensities around 85-95% of
maximal heart rate, with the swim start and bike surges repeatedly exceeding
threshold **[PR]** (draft-legal race-demand studies; the age-group
non-drafting version is steadier but still sits at the top of the sustainable
range). Training that never visits Zone 4-5 does not prepare the athlete for
the event they actually experience — a structural difference from
half-Ironman and Ironman preparation, which is raced almost entirely below
threshold (`tlc §2`).

**Olympic races are tempo/threshold events, not endurance events.** For a
2:30 finisher the effort sits near the top of Zone 3 / bottom of Zone 4 for
the bulk of the race **[PC]**. Durability (the long-course limiter) is rarely
the constraint; sustainable speed is.

Age-group short-course racing is almost always **non-drafting**; the
draft-legal ITU format (surge-and-recover bike demands) concerns elite
athletes and is out of scope for the planner's audience **[PC]**.

## 2. The Intensity Inversion

Relative to long course, short-course preparation **inverts the scarcity**:
volume is easy to satisfy, intensity is the training content that matters.

- The polarized-training evidence base (`em`, Seiler) still applies: the
  *majority* of training time stays low-intensity **[PR]**.
- But the quality fraction is larger and sharper than long course: short-course training holds roughly **15-25% of weekly time at Zone 3+**, with
  Zone 4-5 work appearing weekly in every discipline from the mid-preparation
  onward **[PC]**. Long-course plans concentrate quality in one or two
  sessions; short-course plans distribute smaller doses across more sessions.
- VO2max-type work (short intervals at or above 5-K run / 10-min bike
  effort) is a standing weekly ingredient for MODERATE+ short-course
  athletes, not a BUILD-phase-only specialization **[PC]**. This is the
  opposite polarity of the Ironman finding in `triathlon_long_course.md`.

**[EX]** For the planner's TID tables this means sprint/olympic menus should
carry a visibly higher tempo+interval share than the half-IM menus at the
same tier — if the generated sprint TID is indistinguishable from the
half-IM TID, the menu is wrong.

## 3. Taper

The general taper evidence (`em`; Bosquet et al. meta-analysis) prescribes
**40-60% volume reduction, maintained intensity and frequency** with peak
performance after an 8-14-day taper **[PR]**. Short-course specifics:

- **7-10 days is the practitioner norm** for Olympic; **5-7 days for
  sprint** **[PC]**. The multi-week progressive tapers of Ironman
  preparation (`tlc §5`) do not apply here: short-course tapers run about one week.
- Race week keeps short, sharp touches: openers of 10-30 min per discipline
  with a few race-pace efforts, a rest day two days out, a shakeout the day
  before **[PC]**.
- Frequency is *not* reduced: the taper week still trains most days, at a
  fraction of the duration **[PC]**. A taper that cancels sessions rather
  than shrinking them contradicts the meta-analysis.

**[EX]** Bands derived for the planner: a sprint/olympic race week at
roughly **20-40% of peak-week volume** (the same arithmetic the Ironman
race-week band uses) with **no reduction in session count beyond the race-week
rest-day pattern**.

## 4. Volume Norms by Tier

Weekly volumes by tier **[PC]**:

| Tier | Sprint | Olympic |
|------|--------|---------|
| BASIC (finish) | 3-5 h/wk | 4-7 h/wk |
| MODERATE (perform) | 5-8 h/wk | 6-9 h/wk |
| COMPETITIVE (podium-bound) | 7-10 h/wk | 8-12 h/wk |

Session-count norms: **5-6/wk at BASIC** (2 swims, 2 bikes, 2 runs, one of
them often a brick), **7-9/wk at MODERATE+** **[PC]**. Swim frequency below
2/wk does not maintain feel-for-water even at sprint distance (`triathlon.md`)
**[PC]**.

Long-session ceilings are race-referenced, not marathon-referenced: the
longest ride reaches **1.5-2x race bike distance** and the longest run
**1.2-1.5x race run distance** by mid-preparation **[PC]**. There is no short-course equivalent of the
Ironman century ride; a 2-2.5 h ride is the Olympic ceiling.

## 5. Bricks and Transitions

- **Brick cadence: weekly or near-weekly from the first third of the plan**
  — much denser than long-course plans, because the bike-to-run transition
  cost is proportionally larger in a short race **[PC]**. A brick most weekends from week 3-4 onward is typical.
- Short-course bricks are **short off the bike**: 10-30 min transition runs,
  often with the first 5-10 min at race pace — not the long-course
  race-simulation brick **[PC]**.
- Transition practice itself (mount/dismount, T1/T2 setup) belongs in race-week and race-rehearsal sessions as an explicit skill instruction **[PC]**. The planner encodes this as session notes, not
  sessions **[EX]**.
- One full race-distance rehearsal brick in the final third, at easy pace, is a recurring feature for first-timers **[PC]**.

## 6. Open Water and Race Skills

For BASIC athletes the swim is the highest-anxiety, highest-DNF-risk leg
**[PC]**. Beginners convert one pool swim to open water
roughly every 2-4 weeks once available, practising sighting, wetsuit wear,
and swimming in contact **[PC]**. The planner's aquatic catalog models this
as endurance swims with open-water notes; no separate activity key is
warranted at current menu granularity **[EX]**.

## 7. Recovery Cadence and Progression

- The 3:1 load:recovery week cadence transfers unchanged from `em` **[PC]**.
  Very short horizons omit discrete recovery
  weeks entirely and rely on the low absolute volume **[PC]** — a planner
  horizon rule, not a defect **[EX]**.
- Progression magnitude: ~10-15% weekly volume growth within a block, with
  intensity progressing by session content (zone ladder within a constant
  slot) rather than by added minutes **[PC]**.

## 8. Per-Category Planning Parameters

Compact reference for menu authoring; every figure carries its tier above.

| Parameter | Sprint | Olympic |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Typical preparation horizon | 8-12 wk | 12-16 wk |
| Race duration (planner default) | ~1.5 h BASIC / ~1.25 h MODERATE+ | ~3.0 h BASIC / ~2.5 h MODERATE+ |
| Taper length | 5-7 d | 7-10 d |
| Race-week volume vs peak | 20-40% | 20-40% |
| Peak long ride | 1.5-2x race distance (~60-90 min) | 1.5-2x race distance (~2-2.5 h) |
| Peak long run | 1.2-1.5x race distance (~40-60 min) | 1.2-1.5x race distance (~60-80 min) |
| Brick cadence | ~weekly from wk 3-4 | ~weekly from wk 3-4 |
| Quality share (Z3+, mid-prep onward) | 15-25% of time | 15-25% of time |
| VO2max work | weekly from mid-prep (MODERATE+) | weekly from mid-prep (MODERATE+) |

All horizon/duration figures **[PC]**, quality shares **[PC]**, long-session
multiples **[PC]** , race-week band **[EX]**.

## 9. What This Document Deliberately Does Not Cover

- Draft-legal/ITU-format preparation (elite-only; different bike demands).
- Super-sprint and mixed-relay formats.
- Strength & conditioning dosage — the planner's activity library has no S&C sport.
- Anything already covered sport-generically in `em` / `triathlon.md`.
