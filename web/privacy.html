For the site endury.app. Version of 3 November 2026.

This page says who is responsible for the site, what the site itself processes, what stays in your browser, and what your browser sends to other services on your instruction.

3. What stays in your browser

Nothing is stored or cached until you start the demo or connect, except a theme you pick. Once you do, Endury keeps these in your browser's local storage, none of it sent to the provider of this site:

  • your settings, needed to run the app: your intervals.icu API key and athlete id, your OpenRouter key and choice of AI model if you use chat, service addresses if you changed them, the name you may enter, the version of the chat consent you agreed to, and whether this browser runs the demo;
  • which week and day of the calendar you last had open, so the app reopens there, and the theme you chose;
  • for a moment during OpenRouter sign-in, a one-time code that completes it, and during connecting, a flag for the ready screen.

It also caches the runtime and the site's files once you start, so the about 34 MB download happens only once. The settings and the cache are what it takes to run the app you started (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG); the last place and the theme are small conveniences that stay on this device.

To remove it: Settings → Forget my keys removes your keys and settings. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of it.

4. Your intervals.icu account

Endury works on your own intervals.icu account with the API key you enter, which is only needed for a plan of your own: the demo works without one. Your browser sends that key only to intervals.icu. An intervals.icu API key has full access to your account, with no narrower option. Endury reads your activities, thresholds, fitness and wellness live and stores no copy anywhere else. It writes and removes the planned sessions and races it created, pairs a completed activity with its session, and writes the thresholds you save in Planning Inputs to your sport settings and the feel and effort you enter to the activity. It keeps three notes on your calendar, hidden from your calendar view, that hold your plan, profile, the coach's memory document, the last 50 chat messages and the coach's takes and briefs, so every device you use sees the same plan. These notes are not encrypted: the memory is readable text and the rest is only compressed, so a coach or app you have given access on intervals.icu may be able to read them, including any health details you mention. The provider of this site receives none of it.

All of this runs on your instruction, from your browser, under your own account with intervals.icu Ltd and its own privacy policy.

To remove Endury's data from your account: delete the plan in Endury, which removes its sessions and races from today on. Past events, workouts the coach booked and races added outside a plan stay until you delete them in intervals.icu; events carry the tag endury (older ones coachie). Then delete the notes "Endury data", "Endury chat" and "Endury session takes" in intervals.icu, dated 31 December 2099, and clear this site's data.

5. The coach chat

Chat is optional and off until you add an OpenRouter key and tick the consent box in Settings. Connect takes you to OpenRouter's own sign-in, where its site and terms apply. When you send a message, your browser sends it, with the recent conversation and the coach's working context, to OpenRouter, Inc. (USA), which passes it to the company that runs the AI model. For Endury's default model that is currently Google or Amazon, in the US, the EU or elsewhere; another model you pick may go to another company. The working context is your profile, the memory document (goals, constraints such as injury or availability, preferences, notes), the plan summary, recent training including activity names, which can name a place, the results of the lookups the coach makes, and recent wellness readings: sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and weight, if you record them. GPS tracks reach it only as distances from a shared point, never as coordinates. These wellness readings and anything you write about illness, injury or another health matter are health data in the sense of Art. 9 GDPR. Each call also names Endury as the calling app, which OpenRouter may show in its public rankings; that carries nothing about you.

This happens only on the basis of your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) and Art. 49(1)(a) GDPR), given by ticking the box in Settings, and only under your OpenRouter account and its terms. You can withdraw it at any time by clearing the OpenRouter key in Settings or by Forget my keys; chat then stops and nothing further is sent. Endury records the consent's version in your browser, not anywhere else. The notes in section 4 stay on your account until you delete them.

Endury asks OpenRouter to avoid providers it lists as collecting the content and to use only endpoints it lists as keeping no data. That rests on OpenRouter's lists, which it describes as its best knowledge, and a provider may still keep flagged content under its own terms. OpenRouter keeps metadata about each call under its own terms. The United States has no general adequacy decision, so your data may have less protection there, including from access by authorities.

Their policies: OpenRouter, Google Cloud, Amazon Web Services. Chat is for adults (18 or over). Coach replies, takes and briefs are written by an AI model and are marked as such.
